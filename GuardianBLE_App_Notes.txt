  GuardianBLE — How the App Works
  ES 130 Embedded Systems | BSCS-3A 2026
================================================================


----------------------------------------------------------------
  OVERVIEW
----------------------------------------------------------------

GuardianBLE is a child/pet proximity alarm system that works
entirely over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE). No internet or GPS
is required.

  - ESP32 DevKit V1  : worn by the child/pet, acts as a BLE
                       beacon and GATT server
  - Flutter Android  : runs on the parent's phone, monitors
                       signal strength and triggers alarms

When the child moves too far from the parent:
  1. The phone sounds an alarm and vibrates
  2. The phone sends a BLE command to the ESP32 to activate
     its buzzer as well


----------------------------------------------------------------
  HOW DISTANCE IS ESTIMATED
----------------------------------------------------------------

The app does NOT use GPS. Distance is estimated from the
Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI), which is a measure
of how strong the Bluetooth signal is at the receiver.
RSSI is measured in dBm (decibels relative to 1 milliwatt).
It is always a negative number. The closer to zero, the
stronger the signal (closer the device).

  Examples:
    -45 dBm  →  very close (< 1 m)
    -70 dBm  →  a few meters away
    -90 dBm  →  far away or signal is weak


  Formula used (Free-Space Path Loss model):
  ─────────────────────────────────────────
    d = 10 ^ ( (TxPower - RSSI) / (10 × n) )

  Where:
    d        = estimated distance in meters
    TxPower  = RSSI measured at exactly 1 meter from the
               device = -59 dBm (calibrated for ESP32)
    RSSI     = current averaged signal strength reading
    n        = path loss exponent = 2.5
               (accounts for indoor walls, obstacles, etc.
                free space = 2.0, indoors typically 2–4)
    10 × n   = 25 (the denominator)

  Example calculation at RSSI = -71 dBm:
    d = 10 ^ ( (-59 - (-71)) / 25 )
      = 10 ^ ( 12 / 25 )
      = 10 ^ 0.48
      = ~3.0 meters  ← the SAFE/WARNING boundary

  Example calculation at RSSI = -84 dBm:
    d = 10 ^ ( (-59 - (-84)) / 25 )
      = 10 ^ ( 25 / 25 )
      = 10 ^ 1.0
      = 10.0 meters  ← the WARNING/ALARM boundary


  IMPORTANT LIMITATION:
  RSSI-to-distance is an approximation. Real-world signal
  is affected by walls, body blocking, phone orientation,
  and RF interference. Expect ±1–2 m variance around the
  stated boundaries.


----------------------------------------------------------------
  RSSI SMOOTHING (ROLLING AVERAGE)
----------------------------------------------------------------

Raw RSSI readings are noisy — they can jump 10–20 dBm in a
single scan even if nothing moves. To prevent the status from
flickering, the app uses a rolling average over the last 8
RSSI readings.

  How it works:
    - Each new reading is added to a list (max 8 entries)
    - The oldest reading is dropped when the list is full
    - Status is computed from the average of all readings
      in the list, not from the raw latest reading

  Effect on timing:
    - Going into alarm: takes ~4–5 readings to confirm
    - Coming back to safe: takes ~3–5 readings to confirm
    - At "balanced" scan mode (~2–5s per reading), this
      means transitions take roughly 6–15 seconds

  Fast-recovery flush:
    - If a new reading is 20+ dBm STRONGER than the current
      average, it means the device physically came much
      closer very quickly (e.g., parent runs to child)
    - In this case, the old stale readings are cleared
      immediately so recovery happens in 1–2 readings
      instead of 17+ seconds
    - 20 dBm is used as the threshold because normal RF
      noise is only ±5–10 dBm — a 20 dBm jump is always
      a genuine physical change


----------------------------------------------------------------
  PROXIMITY STATE MACHINE
----------------------------------------------------------------

The app has 4 states per beacon:

  SAFE
    Condition  : averaged RSSI >= -71 dBm
    Distance   : 0 – 3 meters
    Action     : nothing, green indicator

  WARNING
    Condition  : averaged RSSI between -71 and -84 dBm
    Distance   : 3 – 10 meters
    Action     : yellow visual alert on phone only
                 (no sound, no buzzer)

  ALARM
    Condition  : averaged RSSI < -84 dBm
    Distance   : beyond 10 meters
    Action     : phone alarm sound + vibration loop
                 + BLE command sent to ESP32 to activate
                 its buzzer (GPIO 25)

  DISCONNECTED
    Condition  : no signal received for 15 seconds
    Distance   : out of BLE range entirely
    Action     : same as ALARM (phone alarm + vibration)
                 but GATT write to buzzer is skipped
                 because the device is unreachable


  HYSTERESIS (prevents boundary flickering):
  ───────────────────────────────────────────
  The thresholds for entering and leaving a state are
  intentionally different. This prevents the status from
  rapidly switching back and forth when RSSI hovers near
  a boundary.

    Entering WARNING (from SAFE):   RSSI drops below -71 dBm
    Leaving  WARNING (back to SAFE): RSSI rises above -66 dBm
    (need to be at ~1.9 m before WARNING clears, not just 3 m)

    Entering ALARM (from WARNING):   RSSI drops below -84 dBm
    Leaving  ALARM (back to WARNING): RSSI rises above -78 dBm
    (need to be at ~5.75 m before ALARM clears, not just 10 m)


----------------------------------------------------------------
  RSSI THRESHOLD DERIVATION
----------------------------------------------------------------

The -71 dBm and -84 dBm thresholds were calculated to match
the 3 m and 10 m target distances using the inverse formula:

  RSSI = TxPower - (10 × n × log10(d))
       = -59 - (25 × log10(d))

  For d = 3 m:
    RSSI = -59 - (25 × log10(3))
         = -59 - (25 × 0.4771)
         = -59 - 11.93
         = -70.93  ≈  -71 dBm

  For d = 10 m:
    RSSI = -59 - (25 × log10(10))
         = -59 - (25 × 1.0)
         = -59 - 25
         = -84 dBm


----------------------------------------------------------------
  BLE SCANNING BEHAVIOR
----------------------------------------------------------------

The app uses Android BLE scanning in "balanced" mode:
  - Scan window  : 2048 ms active out of every 5120 ms cycle
  - Typical delivery: one batch of results every ~2–5 seconds
  - Chosen for stability — "low latency" mode gives faster
    readings but causes noisy false alarms

Scan restart timer:
  - The scan is stopped and restarted every 20 seconds
  - This is required because Android silently kills
    continuous BLE scans after ~25–30 seconds
  - There is a 500 ms gap during each restart where no
    readings come in

Disconnect watchdog:
  - A timer checks every 3 seconds if any beacon has not
    been seen in the last 15 seconds
  - If so, the beacon is marked DISCONNECTED and the alarm
    triggers


----------------------------------------------------------------
  BLE GATT — HOW THE BUZZER IS CONTROLLED
----------------------------------------------------------------

When the phone decides the alarm should be ON, it connects
to the ESP32's GATT server and writes a single byte:

  Write 0x01  →  Buzzer ON  (ESP32 activates GPIO 25)
  Write 0x00  →  Buzzer OFF (ESP32 deactivates GPIO 25)

  Service UUID      : 4fafc201-1fb5-459e-8fcc-c5c9c331914b
  Characteristic UUID: beb5483e-36e1-4688-b7f5-ea07361b26a8

The phone connects, writes the byte, then disconnects.
It does NOT maintain a permanent GATT connection so that
BLE advertising (used for RSSI distance monitoring) is not
interrupted.

If the beacon is DISCONNECTED (out of range), the GATT
write is skipped — the device is unreachable anyway.


----------------------------------------------------------------
  INACTIVE BEACON FEATURE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Users can mark individual beacons as "Not in use" using the
toggle switch on each beacon card.

  Active   : beacon is monitored; going offline triggers alarm
  Inactive : beacon is ignored; offline does NOT trigger alarm
             and the badge shows "INACTIVE" instead of "OFFLINE"

This is useful when you have multiple registered beacons but
only some of them are currently being used. The inactive state
is saved and persists after the app is closed and reopened.


----------------------------------------------------------------
  BACKGROUND MONITORING
----------------------------------------------------------------

The app continues monitoring even when the screen is off or
the app is minimized. This works through:

  1. Android Foreground Service
       A persistent service (GuardianForegroundService.kt)
       keeps the Dart process alive. Without this, Android
       would freeze or kill the app after a few minutes.

  2. Battery optimization exemption
       The app requests to be excluded from Android's Doze
       mode, which would otherwise suspend BLE scan callbacks
       and Dart timers when the screen is off.

  3. Background notifications
       When a beacon goes to WARNING, ALARM, or DISCONNECTED
       while the app is in the background, a push notification
       is shown on the status bar.


----------------------------------------------------------------
  HARDWARE (ESP32 BEACON)
----------------------------------------------------------------

  Component       GPIO   Notes
  ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Active buzzer    25    HIGH = ON, LOW = OFF
  Push button      26    INPUT_PULLUP; pressing it silences
                         the buzzer locally on the ESP32
  Status LED        2    Built-in LED on DevKit V1
  Power           USB    Laptop or 5V power bank

  BLE advertising interval: 100 ms (ESP32 default)
  This means the phone can receive up to 10 signal readings
  per second from the ESP32 when in range.


----------------------------------------------------------------
  REGISTERED BEACON PERSISTENCE
----------------------------------------------------------------

Beacon names and their active/inactive state are saved to
the phone's local storage (SharedPreferences) as JSON.
They are loaded automatically when the app starts, so you
do not need to re-register beacons every time.

  Saved fields per beacon:
    - deviceId    : BLE MAC address (unique identifier)
    - displayName : user-assigned name (e.g. "Nico")
    - isActive    : whether the beacon triggers alarms
